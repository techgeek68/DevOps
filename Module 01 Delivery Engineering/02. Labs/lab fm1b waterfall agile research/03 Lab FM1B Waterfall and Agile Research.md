# Lab FM1.B: Waterfall Failure and Agile Success Research

---
**Objective**

Understand why development model choice matters by studying real outcomes.

---
**What You Need**

- Internet access
- Text editor or word processor
- Approximately 60 to 90 minutes

---
**Prerequisites**

Read Section F.2 of this module before starting.

---
**Instructions**

Research one real Waterfall project failure and one Agile success story. Write a structured comparison of approximately 400 to 500 words using this structure:

1. Waterfall case summary: what was being built, what went wrong, what it cost

2. Root cause: which specific Waterfall characteristics made this outcome likely

3. Agile case summary: what was being built, how Agile was applied

4. Contrast: how the Agile practices would have changed the Waterfall outcome

5. Conditions where Waterfall is still the right choice today

Include at least two sources per case.

**Suggested cases**

Waterfall failures:

- FBI Virtual Case File and Sentinel (2001 to 2012). Note that these are two separate projects under one program. Virtual Case File ran from 2001 to 2005 under SAIC and was scrapped outright. Sentinel followed, awarded to Lockheed Martin in 2006, and ran as a waterfall effort until the FBI pulled the contract back in 2010 after cost and schedule failures. Cite the phase you are analyzing correctly. If you want a single continuous waterfall failure, use Sentinel 2006 to 2010, not 2001 to 2012.
  Source for the 2010 stop work decision and cost overrun: https://spectrum.ieee.org/fbi-takes-control-of-sentinel-program-

- UK NHS National Programme for IT, dismantled 2011.
  Source: https://committees.parliament.uk/committee/127/public-accounts-committee/news/181704/npfit-report/

- Healthcare.gov initial launch, 2013

Agile successes:

- FBI Sentinel agile rescue phase, 2010 to 2012. Same program as the waterfall case above, which makes it a strong before and after comparison with real cost and schedule numbers on both sides.

  Source: https://www.cio.com/article/286775/agile-development-how-the-fbi-proves-agile-works-for-government-agencies.html

- Amazon's two pizza team structure

- Any documented agile transformation you find credible, verified against at least one primary or audited source, not just a consulting blog

Avoid using Spotify's squad model as your agile success case. It is one of the most cited examples in agile writing, but the coaches and the whitepaper's own co author have since said in public that the model as described was aspirational and was never fully implemented at Spotify.

Source, a former Spotify employee describing this directly: https://www.jeremiahlee.com/posts/failed-squad-goals/

Second source making the same point: https://agilepainrelief.com/blog/the-spotify-model-of-scaling-spotify-doesnt-use-it-neither-should-you/

Using the squad model here will not hold up once you check sources, and the grader will check.
