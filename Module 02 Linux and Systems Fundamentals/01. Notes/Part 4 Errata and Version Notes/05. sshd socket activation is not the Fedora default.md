## `sshd` socket activation is not the Fedora default

The socket activation discussion in Part 3 uses `sshd.socket` as an illustration. The concept is correct, but the defaults differ by distribution, and getting this wrong locks you out of a host:

* **Fedora and RHEL** run `sshd.service`, started at boot. A proposal to drop `sshd.socket` was made and then rejected, precisely because a socket activated SSH daemon can be denied service by exhausting the connection limit, and socket units cannot carry a `Restart=` directive. Use `sshd.service`.

* **Ubuntu 22.10 and later, including 24.04 LTS,** use `ssh.socket` by default. On those systems, changing `Port` in `/etc/ssh/sshd_config` does not change the listening port. You must override `ListenStream=` in a `ssh.socket` drop in, or mask the socket and enable `ssh.service`.

This is worth remembering as a general principle: when the port a daemon listens on does not match its config file, ask who owns the socket.
